## ABSTRACT

There has been explosive growth in the field of decentralised finance (DeFi), and it has created new compliance and security challenges, particularly in detecting illicit fund flows on public blockchains. With Total Value Locked exceeding $3.8 trillion globally as of August 2025, DeFi protocols such as decentralised exchanges, cross-chain bridges, and mixers remain vulnerable to sophisticated money laundering techniques, interaction with sanctioned entities, and the movement of stolen assets.

This dissertation studies the feasibility of a real-time, risk assessment system that evaluates blockchain transactions before on-chain services process them. The framework leverages Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to model transaction graphs and classify patterns of activity as either benign or malicious. A labelled dataset was constructed by tracing over 70 major crypto exploits and contrasting their laundering behaviours, such as peeling chains, scattering, and mixer interactions with legitimate user activity. Rule-based pruning strategies and feature engineering were used to capture structural, behavioural, temporal, and gas-related characteristics of wallet interactions. 

Four machine learning models were evaluated, which include Random Forest, GraphSAGE (Graph Sample and Aggregation), Graph Isomorphism Network (GIN), and Graph Attention Network (GAT). Results show that GNNs substantially outperform traditional approaches, with the GIN achieving the highest detection performance by assigning greater weight to critical high-risk interactions. 

However, production viability analysis reveals that current performance levels fall short of the 99.99% precision threshold required for autonomous transaction blocking in DeFi systems. At Ethereum’s daily volume of 1.5 million transactions, the 5% false positives would create unacceptable disruption for legit users. Consequently, this work suggests the exploration of hybrid AI-human feedback loops and post-transaction analysis frameworks as potential solutions that leverage ML predictions while maintaining user experience in future research.

This work contributes a practical foundation for enhanced DeFi compliance while identifying specific precision improvements necessary for fully autonomous deployment, demonstrating both the potential and current limitations of graph-based machine learning in strengthening financial security for decentralised environments.
